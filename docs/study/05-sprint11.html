5. Sprint 11: launches, O(1) index, packaging, and what failed

Launch overhead and the fused epilogue

Concept. Launching a GPU kernel costs ~5 us of CPU time. v5 did its real work in 2 kernels but needed ~50 more tiny PyTorch ops on [B, C, K] tensors (means, signs, nanmean, class mask, weights, reduction, and an autograd call for the reduction's jacobian). On a 128^3 volume that hides behind GPU work; on the small deep-supervision outputs (32^3, 16^3) the call is launch-bound: its time is the launches.

The fix: one Triton program computes the whole epilogue from the histogram:

O(1) bin index, exactly

Concept. The kernels found a bin by comparing p against all K edges (20 compares, 40 for soft). The edges are almost j / K, so floor(p * K) is within one of the right bin. Two correction steps against the same float32 edge table make it exact:

The same logic on the CPU, checked against torch.bucketize on every float32 within 8 ulp of every edge (the test on the GPU does the same for K = 1..100):

Result on the GPU: the loss got only 1.05-1.2x faster. The profile explains it: the histogram kernel's cost is the [BLOCK, K] reduction (K compares + 3 multiply-sums per voxel), not the index. That reduction is the remaining gap to the bandwidth limit.

The failed experiment: tensor cores

Idea. The reduction is a matrix product: onehot[K, BLOCK] @ values[BLOCK, 3]. Tensor cores do matrix products in fp16 very fast. To stay exact, every fp32 value is split into three fp16 parts (a + b/2^11 + c/2^22); a 0/1 one-hot matrix is exact in fp16.

Result. Correct (tests pass) but 4-8x slower on the T4. Triton uses tensor cores for tl.dot from compute capability 8.0; on the T4 (7.5) the matmul runs on ordinary FMA units and does more work than the plain sums. Kept as v6_dot to re-test on the RTX PRO 6000.

Self-check: Why did the O(1) index help less than expected?
It removed K compares per voxel, but the reduction still builds a [BLOCK, K] one-hot and does 3 multiply-sums over it: that is most of the kernel's arithmetic.
Self-check: Why is the fused epilogue 17-26x faster at 32^3/16^3 but almost no gain at 128^3?
Small calls are launch-bound (~55 launches x 5+ us); large calls are GPU-bound and the epilogue's work is tiny either way.
Self-check: What does register_fake do and why is it needed?
It tells torch.compile the output shapes/dtypes of the op without running it, so the compiler can trace the graph around the opaque op.
